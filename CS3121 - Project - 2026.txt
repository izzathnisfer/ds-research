1. Project Overview
This is a group-based project, with each team consisting of 5 students.
The objective is to identify, design, and implement a real-world data science solution, and to
prepare a research-style paper suitable for submission to a local or international conference.
Students will experience the complete data science lifecycle, including:
● Problem formulation
● Data acquisition and preprocessing
● Analysis and/or machine learning
● Visualization and insight generation
● Research writing and academic dissemination
2. Data Requirements
Groups may use one or more of the following approaches:
2.1. Existing Datasets
● Open datasets (Kaggle, UCI, government portals)
● Public research repositories
● Open APIs (weather, transport, finance, social data)
● Examples
○ LDF Datasets - https://ldflk.github.io/datasets/
○ https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.04124
2.2. Dataset Creation via Integration
● Combine multiple datasets (e.g., education + demographics, transport + weather)
● Address challenges such as:
○ Inconsistent formats
○ Missing values
○ Duplicate records
○ Temporal or spatial misalignment
2.3. New Data Collection
● Surveys
● Sensor-based or log data
● Ethical data scraping (with justification)
Data documentation is mandatory (source, limitations, assumptions, ethics).
3. Analysis & Modelling Expectations
Depending on the problem, projects may include:
3.1. Data Analysis
● Descriptive statistics
● Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)
● Correlation and association analysis
● Hypothesis testing
3.2. Machine Learning (Optional but Encouraged)
● Classification/regression
● Clustering
● Time-series analysis
● Rule mining/pattern discovery
● Model evaluation and validation
Note: Model complexity must be justified. Simple, well-reasoned models are
preferred over unjustified complexity.
4. Visualization Requirements
Students must use both:
● Exploratory visualizations (for analysis)
● Explanatory visualizations (for communication)
5. Code Requirements
● Language: Python (preferred) or as appropriate for the domain
● Documentation: Comprehensive code comments and README
● Reproducibility: All experiments must be reproducible
● Repository: Clean, organized code structure
6. Research Paper Requirement
Each group must write a conference-style research paper, including:
● Abstract
● Introduction & Motivation
● Related Work
● Dataset Description
● Methodology
● Experiments, Results & Analysis
● Discussion & Insights
● Conclusion & Future Work
● References
The paper must be submitted to at least one conference. Teaching Assistants will
provide you with a set of eligible conferences where you can submit the paper.

● Accepted at a high-rank / high h-index conference: 100%
● Accepted at any recognised conference: 95%
Paper Rejected

Paper Never Submitted
● The paper will be reviewed by module lecturers (Phase 1 and Phase 2)
● Marks awarded using the research paper rubric. However, the maximum you
can obtain is 50%.
8. Group Work & Individual Responsibility
Each group must consist of exactly 5 students. Although this is a group project, each
student must make a significant and identifiable contribution. Each student is expected
to contribute meaningfully to at least one stage of the data science process (e.g., data
collection, preprocessing, analysis, modeling, evaluation, or visualization). Mere writing,
formatting, or minor edits alone are not considered sufficient contributions.
Each team must submit a colour-highlighted version of the research paper for grading
purposes, in addition to a clean version for conference submission. Assign one distinct
colour to each team member (five or six colours depending on group size) and use these
colours consistently throughout the document to indicate individual contributions. Students
should highlight the sections to which they made substantial contributions, including text they
wrote, figures or visualizations they created, tables they generated, methodology they
implemented, and experimental analysis they conducted.
Since research writing and technical implementation may be carried out by different team
members, short margin comments should be added where necessary to clarify roles. For
example, a student who implemented a machine learning model may be different from the
student who wrote the corresponding section. In such cases, clearly indicate both
contributions. You may also add short comments to clarify specific technical contributions
and joint contributions (if multiple members worked together).
